The assignments deck

The five assignments walk down the stack: use an agent, replace the runtime with your own loop, optimize around a black-box API, own the serving engine, then optimize the whole stack.

The two scoreboards

Evaluation and cost, and the trade-off between them

Evaluation

Evaluate the whole stack, not the model: leakage enters through prompt, retrieval, memory, tools, scaffold and developer iteration.

A real-world agent has more than three dimensions: capability, cost, speed and agent usability; Assignment 3 is graded on all three of capability, cost and speed, using Pareto dominance.

Lecture 7 · slide 20 Evaluation note · slide 4

Cost

Cost = Users x Sessions / User x Turns / Session x Steps / Turn x Tokens / Step x Price / Token.

Every saving has a counter-cost. Net saving = gross saving - counter-cost, and both must be measured on the same tasks

Lecture 8 · slide 2 Cost note · slide 2

Every saving has a counter-cost, and every benchmark carries a protocol. The two scoreboards are why Assignment 3 is graded on capability, cost and speed at once.

Evaluation and cost are the two scoreboards the course keeps. The trade-off between them is the point of the harness competition.

ConceptWhat the course digest saysWhere it is taught
Cost decompositionCost = Users x Sessions / User x Turns / Session x Steps / Turn x Tokens / Step x Price / Token.Lecture 8 · slide 2
Turn reduction rests on a quadratic factturn k re-reads turns 1..k-1, so total cost grows with the square of turn countLecture 8 · slide 5
Step reductionone call per action (4 model calls) becomes plan-then-batch (2 model calls, 3 tools in parallel)Lecture 8 · slide 6
Token reductiondrop stale results, compact history into state, compress observations keeping errors not full logs, offload big outputs to files, retrieve selectively, isolate exploration in a sub-agent that returns a summaryLecture 8 · slide 7
