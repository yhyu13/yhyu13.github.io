The category shift the lecture opens with: students arrive asking "which model" and are told the interesting problem is how you train and serve it at scale. The course inherits the MaDSys framing - efficiency, reliability, performance, sustainability.

What kind of course this is

A systems course about agents

Background assumed

A graduate computer systems course: one prior graduate-level systems course (operating systems, networking, databases, architecture) plus real systems experience.

Shape of the course

Foundations in the first few classes, then Systems for LLMs & agents for the majority: GPU programming and kernels, batching, scheduling and memory management, load balancing and routing, KV-cache and prefix cache, agent serving systems.

Where the money goes

Inference, not training, is the cost center. The lecture is explicit that throughput is the wrong objective for an agent.

Background poll on the Lecture 1 slides covers operating systems, distributed systems, databases, computer networking, computer architecture, transformer architecture, TP/PP/DP/EP, iteration-level scheduling and pagedAttention, linear attention, GPU programming and the Linux kernel. CUDA is not required; comfort with Python and PyTorch is.

Final project

One problem, one claim, one set of measurements

Two tracks

Product (build an AI system that solves a real problem better than the strongest available alternative) or research (make an original, falsifiable claim). Same deadlines, deliverables and rubric; the research track is held to a higher standard of novelty and evidence.

The gap requirement

Product: run at least three of the closest commercial tools, open-source systems or research prototypes on your own task, give the same input directly to a frontier model, describe how each one fails, and end with the gap in one testable sentence.

Goals ladder

75% is the minimum result you are confident you can deliver even if the main idea fails; 100% is what you plan to deliver; 125% is an extension attempted if you reach 100% early. The blog post reports which level you reached.

Rubric

Four dimensions weighted equally for both tracks: problem and motivation, technical execution, evaluation, reporting. A project whose main idea fails can still earn nearly full credit if the blog explains what you expected, what you measured, and why they differ.

The project is the same skill set as the assignments with the problem inverted: students must choose the evaluation rather than receive it. The full spec is on the course project page.

House rules

Late days, bonus, and the A policy

  • Four late days for the semester

    Counted in whole days and spendable across assignments. A submission one minute late consumes a full day. Once they are exhausted, late work loses 20% of its value per day, to a floor of zero after five days.

  • Bonus, capped at 10% overall

    Optional presentations up to 4% each; course feedback up to 4% - the home page says up to 6%; problems current AI cannot solve at 2% each. An AI-stumper submission needs the exact prompt, the responses from ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini with date and model version, the correct answer with evidence, and a sentence on why the models failed.

  • At most 20% of the class receives an A

    There is no exam, midterm or final. Lectures are not recorded. Auditing is allowed if there is room; auditors do not submit assignments and are not graded.

Auditors do the reading for sessions they attend, do not submit assignments, are not graded, and are not provided computing. Attendance is not tracked for participation; participation is earned for active participation.
